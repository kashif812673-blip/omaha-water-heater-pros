Signs Your Tankless Water Heater Needs Repair in Omaha

A tankless unit usually tells you something is wrong before it quits for good, often with a code on its display. Here are the signs Omaha homeowners call us about, and what each one points to.

Error Codes on the Display

Unlike a tank, a tankless water heater has a control board that shows an error code when it detects a fault. The code is the unit telling you where to look: ignition, flow, venting, or overheating. Do not ignore a repeating code, even if the unit still makes some hot water. Write it down before you call, because it tells us where to start. A tankless water heater showing an error code in an Omaha home is one of the most common reasons people call us.

No Hot Water or Lukewarm Water

When a tankless unit stops making hot water, the cause is usually a failed igniter, a flame sensor fault, a flow sensor that is not registering demand, or a gas supply problem. Lukewarm water that never gets hot often means scale has built up inside the heat exchanger, the coil that transfers heat from the burner to the water. We diagnose which it is before replacing anything.

Cold Water Sandwich (A Few Seconds of Cold Between Hot)

A cold water sandwich is a short burst of cold water in the middle of a hot shower, caused by the gap between the hot water left in the pipe and the moment the burner fires again. A few seconds is normal for how tankless works. But if the cold stretch is long or getting worse, it can signal a flow sensor or ignition-delay problem worth checking.

Strange Noises from the Unit

Clicking during ignition is normal. Rumbling, whistling, or a loud buzzing is not. Rumbling often means scale in the heat exchanger. Whistling can point to a venting restriction or a fan problem. We trace the noise to its source rather than guess.

Water Leaking from the Unit

A tankless water heater leaking in an Omaha home can come from loose fittings, a failed pressure relief valve, or a cracked heat exchanger. Scale buildup over time is a common reason a heat exchanger cracks, which is why hard-water descaling matters here. Shut off the water to the unit and call us. A cracked heat exchanger is one of the few faults that can tip a repair toward replacement.

Can't Keep Up with Demand (System Overload)

If your unit used to deliver enough hot water and now falls short when two fixtures run at once, the usual cause is scale cutting into the heat exchanger's output, not the unit being too small. We check flow rate and descale before anyone concludes the unit cannot handle the home. If the unit was genuinely undersized from the day it went in, we will tell you honestly, but a sudden drop in capacity is almost always a repairable fault.

Common Tankless Water Heater Repairs We Handle in Omaha

These are the tankless repairs that come up most in Omaha homes. Here is what each one involves in plain terms.

Heat Exchanger Descaling (Scale Buildup)

The heat exchanger is the coil that heats the water as it passes through. In Omaha's hard water, minerals coat the inside of that coil as scale, which blocks heat transfer and makes the unit run lukewarm, noisy, and inefficient. Descaling is the process of flushing a cleaning solution through the unit to dissolve that scale. We descale the heat exchanger, restore full heat transfer, and check for any scale-related damage. This is the single most common tankless repair in Omaha. Most manufacturers require annual maintenance to keep the warranty valid, so skipped descaling can cost you warranty coverage as well as performance.

Ignition and Flame Failure Repair

If the burner will not light or lights and then drops out, you have an ignition or flame failure, often shown as a code. The cause is usually a worn igniter, a dirty or failed flame sensor (the probe that confirms the burner is lit), or a gas delivery issue. We replace the igniter or flame sensor, clean the assembly, and confirm the burner lights and holds through a full cycle.

Sensor and Thermistor Replacement

A tankless unit relies on sensors to run safely. The thermistor is a temperature sensor that tells the control board how hot the water is. When a thermistor or other sensor fails, you get wrong temperatures, swinging hot and cold, or a shutdown code. We test the sensors, confirm which has failed, and replace it so the unit reads and controls temperature correctly.

Flow Sensor and Water Flow Issues

The flow sensor detects when you turn on a hot tap and tells the unit to fire. If it fails or clogs, the unit may not start, may run weakly, or may give a tankless water heater low water pressure complaint. Scale and debris are common culprits. We clean or replace the flow sensor and clear the inlet filter so the unit responds to demand properly.

Recirculation Pump Issues

Many Omaha homes pair a tankless unit with a recirculation pump, the pump that keeps hot water ready at the tap so you do not wait for it to arrive. When the pump fails, or its timer or aquastat (the controls that schedule when the pump runs) malfunctions, you get long waits for hot water even though the tankless unit itself is working fine. We diagnose whether the fault is the pump, its controls, or the unit, so you are not paying to fix the wrong thing.

Venting Inspection and Repair

A gas tankless unit must vent combustion gases safely. A blocked, disconnected, or restricted vent can shut the unit down on a safety code and is a carbon monoxide risk. We inspect the venting, clear obstructions, and confirm it is sealed and running correctly.

Gas Supply and Pressure Diagnosis

A gas tankless unit needs a strong, correctly sized gas supply to fire at full output, more than a tank unit draws. When the gas line is undersized or pressure is low, the unit short-cycles, drops the flame, or cannot reach temperature under heavy demand. We measure gas pressure at the unit and diagnose supply problems specific to on-demand units. This is tankless-specific gas diagnosis. For gas faults on a standard tank unit, see our gas water heater repair in Omaha page.

Condensate Drain Issues (Condensing Units)

A condensing unit produces acidic condensate that drains away through a small drain line, and that line can clog. Many condensing units also have a neutralizer, a cartridge that treats the acidic water before it drains. A clogged drain or a spent neutralizer can shut the unit down on a code. We clear the drain and service the neutralizer so the unit runs and drains safely.

How a Tankless Water Heater Works

A tankless water heater has no storage tank. When you open a hot tap, cold water flows into the unit, a flow sensor signals the burner or electric element to fire, and the water heats as it passes through the heat exchanger on its way to your tap. When you close the tap, the unit shuts off. That is why it is called on-demand: it heats water only when you use it, and it never runs out the way a tank does. Understanding this helps explain the repairs, since most tankless faults involve the ignition, the flow sensing, or scale in that heat exchanger rather than a leaking tank.

Tankless units come in two types. Non-condensing units vent hot exhaust directly outside. Condensing units, the more efficient type, squeeze extra heat out of the exhaust through a secondary heat exchanger, which produces an acidic condensate that drains away through a small drain line. Knowing which type you have matters, because condensing units have a drain and neutralizer that need their own service.

Tankless Repair vs. Replacement in Omaha

Most tankless problems are repairable, and a tankless unit can last 20 years or more with maintenance, so repair is usually the right call. Replacement becomes the better option in a few specific cases: a cracked heat exchanger, a unit so old that parts are no longer available, or a pattern of repeated failures across different components. We give you an honest read on which situation you are in. If descaling and a sensor fix will give you years more service, that is what we will recommend, not a new unit.

Why Omaha's Hard Water Hits Tankless Units Hardest

Omaha's tap water, supplied by the Metropolitan Utilities District (M.U.D.), is moderately hard. That hardness is harder on tankless units than on tanks. A tank has a wide chamber where sediment settles on the bottom. A tankless heat exchanger moves water through narrow passages, and those narrow passages scale up faster, which is why a tankless unit in Omaha needs descaling more often than the same unit would in a soft-water city.

As scale builds, it blocks heat transfer, forces the burner to work harder, and can eventually crack the heat exchanger. Annual descaling is the fix, and it is far cheaper than a new unit. When we service a tankless unit, we check for scale and tell you honestly where yours stands.

Tankless Care Through Omaha's Four Seasons

A tankless unit in Omaha faces a different demand in each season. Here is how we keep one healthy through the year.

Spring: Flush and descale the heat exchanger to clear the scale that built up over a winter of heavy use, and clean the inlet water filter that catches debris before it reaches the unit.

Summer: Check flow rate and temperature under lighter demand, the easiest time to catch a unit that is starting to fall behind before you need it most.

Fall: Inspect venting and gas connections before the cold sets in, so the unit is ready for the season when you will lean on it hardest.

Winter: Protect against freezing. Tankless units mounted in garages, unheated utility spaces, or on exterior walls are at real risk in an Omaha winter, and a frozen unit can crack. We check freeze protection and advise on keeping an at-risk unit safe through a cold snap.
