What Water Heater Maintenance Includes in Omaha

A real maintenance visit is more than a quick look. Here is what we do on a standard tank water heater, and why each step matters.

Tank flush. We drain the tank and flush out the sediment that Omaha's hard water leaves behind. Sediment is the mineral grit that settles on the bottom, and clearing it restores heating efficiency and quiets the popping noise it causes.

Anode rod check. The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod inside the tank that corrodes on purpose so the steel tank does not. We check how far it has worn and replace it when it is spent, which is one of the cheapest ways to add years to a tank.

Thermostat, elements, and controls test. We confirm the thermostat is holding the right temperature and that the heating elements (electric) or the gas burner and controls (gas) are working correctly.

Leak and connection check. We inspect the fittings, valves, and the base of the tank for any sign of a leak starting.

TPR valve and pressure check. The TPR valve (the temperature and pressure relief valve, a safety device that releases pressure if it climbs too high) gets tested, along with the overall pressure and the expansion tank where one is fitted.

By the end you have a unit running efficiently and a clear picture of its condition. Call [PHONE] to book a maintenance visit.

Why Omaha's Hard Water Makes Maintenance Non-Negotiable

Omaha's tap water, supplied by the Metropolitan Utilities District (M.U.D.), is hard, around 9 to 10 grains per gallon. That hardness is the single biggest reason water heaters here wear out before their time, and it is why maintenance is not optional in this city.

As the water heats, the dissolved minerals drop out and settle as sediment on the bottom of the tank. That sediment does three things: it insulates the burner or element from the water so the unit burns more energy to heat the same amount, it is what makes the popping and rumbling you hear during a heating cycle, and it speeds up the wear on the anode rod and the tank itself. The harder the water, the faster it happens. In a soft-water city a tank might coast for years without attention, but in Omaha an un-flushed tank is losing efficiency and life every month. Annual maintenance is what holds that off.

How Often Should You Maintain Your Water Heater?

For most Omaha homes, once a year. An annual flush and inspection is the schedule that keeps up with the sediment Omaha's hard water deposits, and it is also what most manufacturers specify to keep the warranty valid. A tankless unit should be descaled yearly as well, for the same reason. A few things shorten that interval: very hard water in your part of the metro, heavy hot water use from a large household, or a unit already several years old. If your tank is new and your water is on the softer end, once a year is plenty; if it is older and working hard, we may suggest a look every six to nine months. We will tell you what fits your unit.

Tankless Water Heater Maintenance

A tankless unit needs maintenance too, just a different kind. Instead of a tank flush it needs descaling, which is flushing a cleaning solution through the heat exchanger to clear the scale that Omaha's hard water builds up in its narrow passages. We also check the inline filter and read the unit's error history for any codes that signal trouble coming. Descaling once a year protects both performance and the manufacturer warranty. Maintenance vs. Repair vs. Replacement

It helps to know which one you actually need, and we will tell you honestly.

Maintenance is preventive: a flush, an anode check, and an inspection on a unit that is working fine, to keep it that way. Repair is for a unit with a specific fault, no hot water, a leak, a failed part, which our [water heater repair in Omaha](/) hub covers. Replacement is the call when the tank itself has corroded or failed, or when a unit past 10 to 12 years is costing more in repairs than it is worth, which our water heater replacement in Omaha page explains. Maintenance cannot fix a failed tank, and it will not turn a 14-year-old unit new again, but on a sound unit it is far cheaper than either of the other two. When we come out, we tell you which situation you are in rather than selling you the most expensive one.
