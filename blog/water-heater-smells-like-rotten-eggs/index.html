Why Does My Water Heater Smell Like Rotten Eggs?

A practical guide from Omaha Water Heater Pros.

Home › Blog › Why Does My Water Heater Smell Like Rotten Eggs?

If your hot water smells like rotten eggs, you are not imagining it, and you are not alone. That sulfur smell is one of the most common water heater complaints, and it usually has a simple explanation.

The smell comes from sulfur compounds in the water. The good news is that in most cases it is a water heater problem, not a problem with your whole water supply, and it can be fixed.

First, Find Out Whether It Is the Heater or the Water

Run only the cold tap for a minute and smell the water. Then run only the hot tap and compare the two.

If both the hot and cold water smell, the problem is in your water supply, and a water heater repair will not fix it. In that case, call your water provider or a water treatment professional.

If only the hot water smells, the problem is inside your water heater. That is the case this guide covers.

Why Hot Water Smells Like Rotten Eggs

Inside a water heater, naturally occurring sulfate in water can turn into hydrogen sulfide gas, which smells like rotten eggs. The reaction is helped along by a type of bacteria that thrives in warm, low-oxygen water inside a tank.

The anode rod plays a part too. The anode rod is a metal rod inside the tank that protects it from rust. Magnesium anode rods, the most common type, can react with sulfates and make the smell stronger.

Heaters that sit unused for a while, or run at lower temperatures, tend to smell worse, because the bacteria get more time to grow.

How to Fix a Smelly Water Heater

Flushing the tank is the first step. Draining and flushing removes sediment and much of the bacteria living in it. In Omaha this matters twice over, because hard water, around 9 to 10 grains per gallon, leaves sediment that gives bacteria a place to settle. See our water heater maintenance guide for what a flush involves.

Swapping the anode rod is the second step. Replacing a magnesium rod with an aluminum-zinc rod often reduces or removes the smell while still protecting the tank from rust.

In stubborn cases, a plumber can disinfect the tank and lines to kill remaining bacteria. This is best left to a professional, because the disinfectant must be fully flushed before the water is used again.

If the smell comes back quickly after a flush, try the rod or the disinfection step next, not another flush. A repeat flush alone rarely solves a smell that keeps returning.

Can You Prevent the Smell Coming Back?

Flush on a schedule. An annual flush keeps sediment and bacteria from building up, and it also helps your heater last longer.

Do not turn the temperature too low. Most manufacturers recommend around 120 degrees, which is hot enough for daily use while keeping energy use in check. Turning it far lower can let bacteria grow more easily.

If your heater is old and the smell keeps returning along with rusty water or leaks, it may be time to talk about replacement rather than another repair.

When to Call a Professional

Call a professional if the smell is in both hot and cold water, if the smell comes with rusty water, if the tank is leaking, or if the smell returns after a flush and a rod change. Any of those signs points to something beyond a simple flush.
