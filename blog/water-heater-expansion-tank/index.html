Does Your Water Heater Need an Expansion Tank?

A practical guide from Omaha Water Heater Pros.

Home › Blog › Does Your Water Heater Need an Expansion Tank?

If you have looked at the top of your water heater lately, you may have noticed a small tank mounted above it or on the wall nearby. That is an expansion tank, and it does a quiet but important job.

This guide explains what an expansion tank does, how to tell whether your home needs one, and what installation looks like.

What Does an Expansion Tank Do?

When water heats up, it expands. In an open plumbing system the extra volume pushes back into the city main. In a closed system it has nowhere to go, so pressure inside your pipes and tank climbs.

An expansion tank is a small tank with a rubber bladder and a cushion of air inside. When pressure rises, water pushes into the tank and compresses the air. When pressure falls, the air pushes water back out. That keeps pressure steady and takes stress off your water heater, pipes and fixtures.

Without one in a closed system, the extra pressure usually escapes through the temperature and pressure relief valve, the safety valve on the side of your tank.

How Do You Know If Your System Is Closed?

Many homes become closed systems without the owners realizing it. A check valve on the water meter, a pressure reducing valve, or a backflow preventer all stop water from flowing back into the main, which closes the system.

Newer meters in many cities include check valves, so a home that was open years ago may be closed now. The only way to be sure is to have a plumber check your setup.

Signs You Might Need an Expansion Tank

The relief valve drips or discharges water regularly, especially after the heater runs a heating cycle.

You have replaced the relief valve more than once and it keeps failing.

Your water pressure seems to swing, running high after the heater runs and normal at other times.

Any of these signs is worth a check. A dripping relief valve should never be ignored or plugged, because it is a safety device. If your valve is dripping, get it looked at promptly.

What Does Installation Involve?

The tank is sized to match your water heater and your home water pressure, then mounted on the cold water line near the heater and pre-charged with air to match your house pressure.

Installation is usually a short job for a plumber, and it is often done at the same time as a new water heater installation.

A failed expansion tank can cause the same symptoms, so if you already have one and the relief valve starts dripping, the tank itself may need checking or replacing.

Does Every Home Need One?

No. Homes with a truly open system do not build up the same pressure, so a tank adds little. Homes with a closed system benefit the most.

Local requirements vary, so if you are unsure, ask a plumber to check whether your system is open or closed before deciding.
