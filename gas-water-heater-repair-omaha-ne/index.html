Common Gas Water Heater Problems We Fix in Omaha

A gas unit usually tells you something is wrong through one of a few symptoms. Here is what each one points to.

No hot water. On a gas unit this almost always means the pilot light has gone out, the thermocouple has failed, or the gas control valve is bad. When the pilot is out, the thermocouple shuts off the gas as a safety measure, so no hot water follows. We find which of the three it is before replacing anything.

Not enough hot water. Usually sediment. Omaha's hard water leaves mineral buildup on the bottom of the tank, right over the burner, so the unit heats less efficiently and runs out faster. A failing burner can also be the cause. A flush often restores a unit that is otherwise sound.

Pilot light will not stay lit. A flame that lights and then drops out is usually a failing thermocouple, not a stubborn pilot. A dirty pilot assembly or a draft in an older Omaha home can also be behind it.

Popping or rumbling noises. That sound is water trapped under a layer of hardened sediment, boiling as the burner fires below it. It is both a sign of lost efficiency and a reason to flush the tank.

Water is cooler than it should be. Often the thermostat setting or a failing thermostat, and sometimes a burner that is not firing fully. We check the setting first, then the burner and controls.

Gas Water Heater Components We Repair in Omaha

When a gas unit fails, it is almost always one of these parts. Here is what each one does, in plain terms.

Pilot light assembly. The small standing flame (or on newer units, the electronic igniter that lights the burner) that starts the main burner. Dirt, soot, or a draft can keep it from holding.

Thermocouple. The safety sensor that sits in the pilot flame and keeps the gas valve open only while the flame is lit. When it fails, the gas shuts off and you lose hot water. It is one of the most common gas repairs.

Gas control valve. The part that regulates gas to the burner and responds to the thermostat. A failed valve can leave you with no heat or a unit that will not hold a flame.

Burner. The component that actually heats the water. Sediment and soot can foul it, and it can wear out over years of use.

Igniter. On electronic-ignition units, the part that sparks the burner to life in place of a standing pilot.

Thermostat. Sets and holds the water temperature; a faulty one gives you water that is too cool or swings hot and cold.

We carry the common parts, so most of these are a one-visit repair.

Gas Safety: When to Call Immediately

Some situations are not a repair call, they are a safety call, and they come first.

If you smell gas near your water heater, treat it as serious. Do not touch any switch, flame, or the unit itself, because a spark can ignite gas. Leave the house, and from a safe place away from the home call your gas utility first, then call us. A gas smell is never something to wait on or troubleshoot yourself.

Never do gas-line work yourself. Connecting, moving, or repairing a gas line is licensed work for good reason, and a mistake carries a fire and carbon monoxide risk. The same goes for a burner that is burning yellow or sooty instead of blue, which can signal a combustion problem. Leave gas-line and combustion work to a licensed professional, and call [PHONE] if you are unsure.

Repair or Replace Your Gas Water Heater?

Most gas faults are repairable, and a repair is usually the right call on a sound tank. Gas tank water heaters typically last 8 to 15 years, with high-efficiency models at the upper end. Replacement becomes the better option when the tank itself is leaking or rusting from the inside, or when a unit well into that age range is stacking up repairs. A failed pilot, thermocouple, gas valve, or burner on an otherwise good tank is a repair, not a reason to replace.
