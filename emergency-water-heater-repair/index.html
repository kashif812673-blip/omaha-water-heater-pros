What to Do Right Now

Before we arrive, a few quick actions can stop a small failure from becoming a flooded basement or a safety risk. Find your situation below.

Your Water Heater Is Leaking

Shut off the cold-water supply valve on top of the unit to stop water feeding the tank. Then kill the power: on a gas unit turn the gas control to off, and on an electric unit switch off its breaker. Then call [PHONE]. Do not keep running an electric unit after the water is off, because a heating element left dry burns out in minutes. If water is actively spreading, move anything valuable off the floor.

No Hot Water

First rule out the simple causes: on an electric unit, check whether its breaker has tripped and reset it once; on a gas unit, check whether the pilot light is out. If the breaker trips again, or the pilot will not stay lit, stop there and call [PHONE]. Repeatedly resetting a tripping breaker or relighting a failing pilot can make things worse.

The Unit or Basement Is Flooding

Shut off the cold-water valve on top of the unit. If water is pouring out faster than that stops it, shut off the main water supply to the house. Kill the gas or breaker to the unit, then call [PHONE]. A burst tank will not reseal, so the goal now is to stop the water.

You Smell Gas Near the Unit

Treat this as the most serious. Do not touch any switch, flame, or the unit. Leave the house, and from a safe place away from the home call your gas utility first, then call us. A gas smell is never a wait-until-morning problem.

Emergency Water Heater Repairs We Handle

When you call for emergency water heater repair in Omaha, these are the failures we fix, usually on the same visit.

No hot water. Often a failed thermocouple (the safety sensor that keeps a gas burner lit) on a gas unit, or a burned-out heating element on an electric one.

Leaks. From a connection, a failed temperature and pressure relief valve (the TPR valve, the safety valve that releases pressure from the tank), a worn drain valve, or the tank itself.

Burst tanks. When the tank has failed, no repair reseals it, so this becomes a same-day replacement. See our water heater replacement in Omaha page.

Gas control problems. A failed gas control valve (the part that regulates gas to the burner) that leaves you without heat or will not hold a flame.

Electrical failures. Tripping breakers, failed elements, or thermostat faults on an electric unit.

Why Water Heaters Fail at the Worst Time

Water heaters rarely fail on a mild afternoon. They fail when they are working hardest, which in Omaha means the dead of winter. In a cold snap the incoming water is far colder, so the unit runs harder to keep up, and a tank that was already tired gives out. Omaha's hard water, around 10 grains per gallon, is the slower cause: minerals settle as sediment and wear out the anode rod (the sacrificial rod that corrodes in the tank's place to protect the steel), which shortens the tank's life and leads to the leaks and failures that become emergencies. Age is the third factor, since most tanks last 8 to 12 years and the risk climbs steeply after that. None of this is in your control at 2 a.m., which is exactly why we run 24-hour water heater repair in Omaha.
