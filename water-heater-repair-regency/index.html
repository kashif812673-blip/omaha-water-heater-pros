Why 1972 Matters for Your Water Heater

The median Regency home was built in 1972, and the build years cluster tightly around it: roughly 21 percent of homes went up in the 1950s, 21 percent in the 1960s, nearly 23 percent in the 1970s, and another 12 percent in the 1980s. That distribution is the whole story for water heaters here, and it splits the neighborhood into two service profiles.

The 1970s and 1980s homes are the heart of the replacement window right now. A water heater lasts a typical 10 to 15 years, so any original or long-serving tank in these homes is well past that, which makes Regency a replacement-heavy neighborhood and makes us honest about it: on a sound tank we repair, but on an old unit stacking up repairs, a replacement is the smarter spend. The 1950s and 1960s homes are a different case, old enough that the plumbing can be from the galvanized-to-copper transition era. Galvanized steel supply lines, the silver-gray pipes that corrode from the inside out and restrict flow, speed up tank corrosion, so where we find them we check the lines alongside the tank. And across all of it, Omaha's hard water, around 9 to 10 grains per gallon, drives sediment into the tank, which is why a popping, rumbling unit and an annual flush are part of the conversation here too. Sizing and Upgrading in Regency's Larger Homes

Regency homes are large, and they use hot water accordingly. The original unit, or a like-for-like replacement, is often undersized for how the household actually lives, which is why one of the most common Regency calls is simply running out of hot water. A repair will not fix a sizing problem.

We right-size the system to the home: a larger or high-recovery tank that keeps up with demand, or a tankless unit, which heats water on demand so it does not run out no matter how many fixtures run at once. In a home at this level, a high-efficiency or tankless upgrade is an investment in the property, not an upsell, and we present it that way, with the numbers and the trade-offs, so the choice is yours. We size it to how your household uses hot water, so the shortage does not return.
